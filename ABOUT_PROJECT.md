# About RoTerra

RoTerra is an explainable crop-rotation decision-support tool designed to help farmers explore how they can adapt their farming practices to changing environmental conditions.

The name combines **rotation** and **terra**, meaning earth, reflecting the project's focus on farming decisions informed by an understanding of the land.

## The Problem

Farmers must make decisions about what to grow next while navigating changing rainfall, rising temperatures, water availability, and concerns about soil health. Environmental data can help inform those decisions, but data alone does not explain which options are worth considering or what tradeoffs they involve.

RoTerra aims to bridge that gap: connecting environmental evidence with practical, understandable crop-rotation comparisons.

## What RoTerra Does

RoTerra brings together NASA Earth observations, local agricultural information, crop characteristics, and farmer priorities to help users compare possible rotation strategies.

Rather than presenting a single answer without explanation, it is designed to help farmers:

- Understand environmental conditions and longer-term changes affecting their location.
- Compare their current farming practice with alternative crop rotations.
- Explore tradeoffs involving water, soil health, income potential, pest management, and practical constraints.
- Adjust priorities to reflect what matters most to them.
- See the evidence, assumptions, limitations, and information gaps behind each comparison.

The goal is not to identify one universally best rotation. It is to make the available options easier to understand and discuss.

## Global Vision, Local Demonstration

RoTerra has a global ambition, with an initial demonstration focused on paddy farming in Sekinchan, Selangor, Malaysia.

Sekinchan provides a concrete setting for exploring how environmental information and local farming considerations can inform rotation choices. It is the project's first demonstration case, not its permanent geographic boundary.

The broader vision is to support additional regions through locally appropriate crop choices, soil information, farming practices, and language. Expansion would require local adaptation and review; the project does not assume that recommendations developed for one location are suitable everywhere.

## Meaningful Use of NASA Data

NASA data is intended to inform the comparison, not simply decorate the interface. Each selected dataset should have a clear purpose: revealing a relevant environmental condition, informing a suitability assessment, or making an important risk or uncertainty visible.

RoTerra prioritises relevant, explainable use of data over the number of datasets included.

## Supporting Decisions, Not Replacing Judgment

RoTerra is a decision-support prototype, not a guarantee of crop performance, income, or agricultural suitability. Its comparisons should distinguish observations from assumptions and clearly label illustrative values or missing information.

Demonstrating the tool with Sekinchan data does not constitute field validation. Farmer feedback and local agronomic review would be needed before treating its suggestions as practical farming advice.

The project is intended to support conversations between farmers, agricultural advisers, and other local stakeholders—not replace their experience or judgment.

## Project Context

RoTerra is being developed for the NASA Space Apps Challenge under the challenge **Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data**.

Its central idea is simple: turn environmental evidence into clearer crop-rotation choices, while keeping farmer priorities, local context, and uncertainty visible.
